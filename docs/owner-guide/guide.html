1

What your website does

Pin High UAE is a trade catalogue with a quote-request form. Buyers choose kit and sizes, place their logo on it, and send you a request. You price it and reply. Nothing is charged online and no stock is reserved until you approve.

The journey of a request

1. Buyer browsesCatalogue and product pages, live availability per size.
2. Builds a size runQuantities per size, logo placed on the photo.
3. Sends a requestCompany, contact, delivery, notes, logo files.
4. The siteSaves it, emails your team a summary and spreadsheet, confirms to the buyer.
5. YouPrice it, reply, and mark it Quoted → Approved / Won or Lost.
The buyerThe website, automaticallyYou, in the admin panel

What a buyer sees on a product

Important

The words "request", "not an order" and "nothing has been charged" are deliberate. A buyer who believes they have ordered is the most expensive misunderstanding in this business. Keep that language in anything you send.

2

Signing in for the first time

Every person has their own email, password and an authenticator app on their phone. The first sign-in sets the app up. It takes about two minutes.

Before you start

  • Your email address and the starting password (from whoever set the site up).
  • Your phone, with an authenticator app installed: Google Authenticator or Microsoft Authenticator (free, from the App Store or Google Play). If you already use 1Password or Bitwarden, those work too.
  • Somewhere safe to keep eight recovery codes — a password manager, or a printout in a locked drawer.

Step by step

  1. Go to your site's address followed by /admin. You'll see the sign-in page.
  2. Enter your email 1 and starting password 2, then Sign in 3.
  3. Because no authenticator is set up yet, you are taken straight to the Security page.
  1. On your phone, open the authenticator app and choose Add (usually a +), then Scan a QR code. Point the camera at the square 1.
  2. Can't scan? Choose Enter a setup key in the app, give it the name Pin High UAE, and type the key shown at 2.
  3. The app now shows a six-digit number for Pin High that changes every 30 seconds. Type the current one at 3 and press Turn on 4.
  1. Save the eight recovery codes now. Use Print or Copy all and put them somewhere safe. They will not be shown again.
  2. Tick I've saved these somewhere safe, then Continue to the dashboard.
  3. Still on the Security page later: change the starting password to one only you know (Chapter 12).
Careful

Without recovery codes, a lost phone means asking another owner to reset you — and if you are the only owner, it means a developer. Keep the codes.

3

Signing in every day

Two steps: password, then the six-digit code from your phone. You stay signed in for 12 hours.

Email + passwordOn the sign-in page.
Six-digit codeFrom the authenticator app. Use the newest one.
Signed inFor 12 hours, then sign in again.

Things worth knowing

  • The code changes every 30 seconds. If one is rejected, wait for the next and try that.
  • A code can only be used once — that's normal, not a fault.
  • After too many wrong attempts you're paused for a short while. Wait, then try again.
  • Sign out is top-right, on every admin page.
  • Your email address in the top bar is a link to your own Security page.
Tip

Add the sign-in page to your phone's home screen and your browser's bookmarks. The address is your site's address plus /admin.

5

Handling a quote request

A request is a lead. The panel keeps every line, size and logo the buyer sent, and tracks where you are with it.

When a request arrives

  1. Everyone on the Recipients list gets an email: a summary plus a spreadsheet of the lines. The buyer gets a confirmation with their reference.
  2. It appears on the dashboard under Awaiting a response and in Quote requests with the status New.
  3. Open it, price it, and send the buyer your quote by email as you normally would. Then set the status so the team can see where it is.

Statuses, and what touches stock

NewJust arrived.
In progressSomeone is on it.
QuotedPrice sent to the buyer.
ApprovedBuyer said yes. Stock is taken here.
WonDelivered and done.
Lost · Cancelled · ExpiredNever touch stock.
Changed your mind? Moving a request back off Approved puts the units back into stock automatically.

Editing a request

Lines, quantities and notes can be corrected after a request lands — until stock has been taken. If you need to change an Approved request, move it back to Quoted first, edit, then approve again.

Important

Anything sitting in New for more than 24 hours is flagged on the dashboard. The buyer was promised a response time on their confirmation (you set it under Settings → Quoting) — keep to it.

Tip

Resend notifications on a request re-sends the emails — useful after fixing email sending, or when a new colleague joins the Recipients list.

6

Keeping stock current

The size grid buyers see comes from your last stock upload, minus anything approved since. Upload after every adidas delivery.

Uploading a delivery

  1. Go to Stock. Choose the adidas implementation file (the template with articles and sizes) and the invoice (the quantities). Both are the spreadsheets adidas sends you.
  2. Press upload. The site reads the files and shows a preview: new styles, quantities that will change, and anything it could not match to a known article.
  3. Read the preview. If a column was picked up wrongly, fix the mapping on screen — the site remembers your choice for next time.
  4. Confirm. Quantities go live on the public size grids immediately. The dashboard shows the upload time.
  5. Single sizes can be adjusted by hand from the same page (a damaged item, a sample given away). Every adjustment is recorded in History with your name.
Careful

A wrong upload shows buyers stock you don't have. Read the preview before confirming; if it has gone live, roll it back from Stock → Uploads rather than correcting sizes one by one.

Products and photos

Products arrive from the stock upload. Here you decide what buyers see and how it looks.

Common jobs

To…Do this
Hide a style from buyersTick it and choose Hide. It stays in the panel and in history; it just isn't listed. Untick to show it again.
Add or replace photosOpen the product and upload images. They are resized and optimised automatically. The first image is the one on the catalogue card.
Correct a name, colour or categoryOpen the product and edit the fields. The article number itself never changes — it is the link to the adidas files.
Deal with "needs review"A new import the site couldn't categorise is held back from the public catalogue until you set its category.
Show pre-owned or ex-display stockOff by default. Turn it on under Settings; anything not new is clearly labelled wherever it appears.
Important

Buyers never see wholesale or cost prices anywhere — not on the site, not in the emails, not in the Excel download. Retail RRP is shown, labelled as retail, so a buyer can see the product's normal shelf price without mistaking it for your quote.

Email: sending, and who gets notified

Email is how requests reach you. Two separate settings: the account the site sends from, and the people who receive.

A. The account the site sends from

The site sends through a Gmail account you own, using a special 16-character app password that Google creates for exactly this purpose. Your real Gmail password is never used or stored.

  1. Use a Gmail account for the business — a dedicated one such as pinhigh.quotes@gmail.com is ideal.
  2. In that Google Account: Security → 2-Step Verification (turn it on if it isn't) → App passwords. Create one, name it "Mail" or "Pin High". Google shows a 16-character code once.
  3. In the admin panel: Settings → Email sending. Enter the Gmail address and paste the 16-character code. Save Gmail details. It is stored encrypted and never shown again.
  4. Type your own address under Send a test to and press Send test email. If it arrives, you're done — the status card turns green.
Tip

If Google won't show "App passwords", 2-Step Verification isn't on yet for that account. Turn it on first; the option appears afterwards.

B. Who receives quote requests

  1. Go to Recipients. Under Add someone, enter a name and email address and press Add recipient.
  2. Untick someone to pause them (holiday cover), or Remove them.
  3. Keep at least two active addresses so a single full inbox never loses a lead.
Important

If the dashboard says a notification failed, the request is safe in the panel — nobody was told. Fix email sending (A above), then open the request and press Resend notifications.

Editing your public website

Everything a visitor reads on the home page, the contact details, and the promise you make on confirmations — all under Settings. Changes go live the moment you save.

Also under Settings

  • New-product carousel — a strip of chosen article numbers on the home page. Off until you turn it on.
  • Pre-owned and ex-display stock — hidden from buyers by default (Chapter 7).
  • Check your work: after saving, open the public site in a new tab (View site, top of every admin page) and read it as a visitor would.

When something isn't right

Start with the System status card on the dashboard. Then find the symptom below.

Who fixes what

In the admin panel — you

  • Email sending and the test email
  • Who receives requests
  • Who can sign in, roles, resets
  • Your password and authenticator
  • Stock uploads, rollbacks, adjustments
  • Products, photos, visibility
  • Home page, banner, contact details, response time
  • Every quote request

In the hosting — your developer

  • Any status line marked "Needs your developer"
  • Connecting pinhighuae.com to the site
  • An owner locked out with no phone, no codes and no other owner
  • New features, new brands' file formats, how prices are shown

Your site runs on Vercel; the database and files are on Supabase; the code is on GitHub. All should be registered to the business.

People and access

Owners manage who can sign in under Users. Nobody else — no developer — needs to be involved.

RoleCan do
StaffEverything day to day: quote requests, stock, products, recipients, settings, their own security.
OwnerAll of that, plus this page: invite and remove people, change roles, issue password resets, reset a lost authenticator.

Adding someone

  1. Go to Users. Under Add someone, enter their email 2 and choose Staff or Owner 3. Press Send invitation 4.
  2. They receive an email with a link. It works once and lasts seven days. They choose a password, then set up their authenticator exactly as in Chapter 2.
  3. Until they've done that, they show as Invited — not yet set up in the list 1.

Everything else on this page

LinkWhat happens
Remove accessTheir sign-in stops working immediately, even mid-session. Nothing they did is deleted. Restore access undoes it.
Make owner / Make staffChanges their role. The last owner can't be demoted — make someone else an owner first.
Send password resetEmails them a one-hour link (or shows it to you if email isn't set up). Their authenticator is untouched.
Reset authenticator (lost phone)Removes their authenticator. Next time they sign in with their password, they set up a new one.
Important — always two owners

An owner is the only person who can reset another's lost authenticator. If the only owner loses their phone and their recovery codes, the business is locked out until a developer intervenes. Make a trusted second person an owner today; the status card will remind you until you do.

12

Your own security

Your password and authenticator live under Security (or click your email in the top bar).

Changing your password

  1. Under Admin password, type your current password, then the new one twice. At least 12 characters — a short phrase is easier to remember and harder to guess than a "clever" short one.
  2. Change password. Anyone signed in stays signed in until their 12 hours run out.

Recovery codes

  • The page shows how many you have left. When you're down to two, press Get new recovery codes — a fresh set of eight replaces the old ones (which stop working).
  • Treat them like keys: anyone holding a code and your password can sign in.

New phone

  1. Before you wipe the old phone, sign in on a computer and choose New phone? Replace the authenticator. Enter your password, scan with the new phone and confirm a code. The old phone keeps working until you do.
  2. Old phone already gone? Sign in with a recovery code instead, then do the same.
13

Lost phone, forgotten password

Every one of these has a route back in that does not involve a developer — as long as there are two owners and you kept your recovery codes.

I've forgotten my password
Email is set up on the site
  1. Sign-in page → Forgotten your password?
  2. Enter your email. A link arrives; it works once and lasts an hour.
  3. Choose a new password. You'll be asked for your authenticator code as usual.
Email isn't set up, or the link never arrives
  1. Ask another owner.
  2. They go to Users → Send password reset on your account.
  3. The panel shows them the link; they send it to you on WhatsApp.
I've lost my phone (or it was wiped)
I have my recovery codes
  1. Sign in with your password.
  2. At the code step, enter one recovery code.
  3. You land on Security: choose Replace the authenticator and scan with the new phone.
I don't have the codes
  1. Ask another owner.
  2. They go to Users → Reset authenticator on your account.
  3. Sign in with your password; you'll be taken straight to set up a new authenticator.
I'm the only owner, and I have neither phone nor codes
This one needs a developer

The procedure is in the project's handover document (HANDOVER.md, "Break-glass procedures"). It takes a developer a few minutes. It is also exactly why the site keeps asking for a second owner.

A rhythm for running the site

Ten minutes a day keeps everything current. Here is a routine that covers all of it.

Every day
  • Open the dashboard.
  • Anything red? Deal with it.
  • Reply to requests in New; set their status.
Every week
  • Requests stuck in In progress or Quoted — chase or mark Lost.
  • Glance at "sizes down to single figures".
Every delivery
  • Upload the adidas implementation file and invoice.
  • Read the preview. Confirm.
  • Check a product page.
Every quarter
  • Users: still the right people? Two owners?
  • Recovery codes: enough left?
  • Home page copy and photos still current?

Handover checklist

Tick these once and the business is self-sufficient:

☐The owner has signed in, set an authenticator, and saved the recovery codes.
☐The owner has changed the starting password to one only they know.
☐A second trusted person has been invited as an owner and has finished setting up.

☐At least two addresses are on the Recipients list.
☐A test request has been sent from the public site and arrived by email.

☐The Vercel, Supabase, GitHub and Gmail accounts are registered to the business, and two people know where the logins are.

Glossary

Admin panelThe private part of the site at /admin where you run things. Buyers never see it.
Authenticator appA phone app (Google Authenticator, Microsoft Authenticator) that shows a six-digit code that changes every 30 seconds. The second step of signing in.
Recovery codeOne of eight single-use codes that replace the phone code if the phone is lost.
Owner / StaffThe two roles. Owners can also manage people.
Quote requestWhat a buyer sends: lines, sizes, logo, details. A lead, not an order.
ReferenceThe request's number, e.g. PH-Q-2026-0001. On the confirmation, in every email, in the panel.
Size runQuantities per size for one style and colour.
Article numberadidas's code for a style and colour, e.g. HZ6893. Never changes.
Implementation file / invoiceThe two spreadsheets adidas sends with a delivery. The first lists articles and sizes; the second, quantities.
Retail RRPadidas's recommended shelf price in AED, shown to buyers and labelled as retail. Not your quote.
RecipientsThe email addresses that receive each new request.
App passwordA 16-character code Google creates so an application can send from a Gmail account without knowing the real password.
System statusThe dashboard card that reports each connection the site relies on, and who fixes it.
Vercel / Supabase / GitHubWhere the site runs, where its data and files live, and where its code is kept. Developer territory; owned by the business.
